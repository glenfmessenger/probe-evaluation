# GATES_REPORT.md — three-arm validation of the AASE probe families

Branch `gates`, from `phase2-obfuscation` @ 4e4998d. Every number below comes from a JSON file on this branch carrying a
provenance block (git commit, config hashes, dataset hashes, package versions, GPU) that the runner validated before
committing, and every table is generated by `python -m gates.b4_report` from those files. Nothing is retyped.

Thresholds were pre-registered in `GATES.md` before any GPU work and are unchanged. Six amendments were recorded during
the session; each preserves the original wording struck through, with the new wording and the evidence beside it. Two
amendment *types* are distinguished (DECISIONS.md #10): **type A, mechanism reassigned** — the threshold is met and the
effect is real, but its pre-registered cause is refuted by a control; **type B, evidence disqualified** — the threshold
is met but the evaluation data cannot bear the claim.

## Verdicts

Two controls were applied to every arm by default, because this project has been burned by both: every AUC is reported
raw and length-stratified, and detection is reported conditioned on whether the model itself refused. A third was added
during the session and is now standing policy (DECISIONS.md #11): every APC table carries a lexical-baseline column, and
AF carries one too.

### A1.5 What Arm 1 establishes

Two findings retire both halves of the paper's headline methodological contribution.

**The estimator identity (Amendment 2).** For complete, aligned, equal-sized pairs, the paper's paired rule
v = mean_i(h_i^+ - h_i^-) and its standard rule v = mean(h^+) - mean(h^-) are *the same estimator*: the mean of
within-pair differences is identically the difference of class means. The two rows agree to the last bit on every model
and mode, and the output asserts it. No experiment can separate them, so "paired contrastive training" is not a distinct
training method.

**The off-topic ablation (Amendment 3, type A).** What the paper attributes to pairing must then come from the paired
*data*. It does not. Holding the harmful side and the estimator fixed and swapping only the negative set — on-topic
twins against the off-topic prompts the shipped probes were trained on — moves AUC vs JailbreakBench benign by between
-0.003 and +0.018. Reading the identical text through the model's own chat template instead moves length-stratified AUC
by +0.296 to +0.544, positive on all seven models.

**The surviving constructive claim, made for AF only:** the topic confound Phase 1 found is a property of the extraction
convention — reading the activation at the final token of bare text — and largely disappears when the prompt is read
through the model's own chat template. Phase 1's AUC vs JailbreakBench of 0.46-0.67 with 30-94 % false positives becomes
0.87-0.93 with 0-7 % at a matched operating point. The scope is deliberate (Amendment 4): "templated" means a different
operation for Arm 2's agent transcripts and Arm 3's model responses, so their mode comparisons are reported as
observations, not as tests of this hypothesis.

The probe also clears the lexical bar. The strongest cheap text baseline reaches 0.710 length-stratified overall and
0.637 against JailbreakBench benign; the paired templated probe beats it by +0.213 to +0.272. Worth noting for the
paper: the single best *raw* lexical feature is prompt length at 0.903, which is exactly the trap that invalidated the
Phase 2 obfuscation numbers, and is why the length-stratified column is the one that counts.

### A2.5 What Arm 2 establishes — the strongest constructive result of the session

Phase 1 read the AAG probe at the final token of the agent scaffold and measured AUC 0.52-0.72 with no usable operating
point. The injection is not at that position: it sits at a median 85 % of the tool-response span and never at its end.
Reading the mean of the tool-response span instead — fully deployable, no label knowledge — reaches **0.94-0.97** on
four of seven models (0.95-0.97 on three), against 0.55-0.86 for the final-token read trained on the same data.

**The result is generalisation, not memorisation.** The pre-registered split shares all 62 attacker instructions and all
17 user instructions across halves, because 1,054 cases are built from that small a pool. That split measures
recognition. A grouped split assigning whole injection strings to one side, so no injection in the test half was ever
seen in training, reproduces the result: the mean AUC drop across models is between -0.016 and +0.005 for every design.
This is the headline number.

The oracle read, which is handed the injection position and is never deployable, reaches 0.96-0.99. The gap between it
and the best deployable read is +0.02 to +0.15 on five models and negative on two, so most of the available signal is
already recoverable without label knowledge. The three-layer check moves the oracle by at most 0.09 and is reported as a
note, not as a new headline layer.

Refusal separation shows the probe is not redundant with the model's own behaviour here: the models refuse only 3-25 %
of injection prompts and follow the injected instruction in 2-5 % of generations, so there is little behavioural signal
for the probe to be re-detecting.

**The sentence the paper may write:** on real InjecAgent data, a linear probe on the mean activation over the
tool-response span detects prompt injection at AUC 0.94-0.97 on the best four of seven models, on injection strings
never seen in training, where the same probe read at the final prompt token reaches 0.58-0.86 — the Phase 1 result was
an extraction-position artefact, and the corrected read is deployable. (Per-case split, for the like-for-like comparison
against Phase 1's convention: mean-read 0.95-0.97 on three models, final-token 0.55-0.85.)

### A3.5 What Arm 3 establishes

**The boundary result stands, and it is unconditional.** The shipped January APC probe has no external validity. Its
medical AUC on the external set is 0.14-0.58 across models — at or below chance — and at its own threshold it flags
essentially everything, including **every advice case, every information case and all 20 refusals in four of the six model/mode cells**, which are precisely the
responses the policy is trying to elicit. The paper's reported AUC of 1.00 was an artefact of training on 15 short
authored pairs and testing on a six-example split. That is withdrawn.

**The constructive half is withheld (Amendment 5, type B), and now permanently for authored short text.** Freshly
trained probes reach 0.87-0.99 medical and 0.96-1.00 cross-topic, clearing the pre-registered thresholds. But the
evaluation data cannot bear the claim:

- counting second-person pronouns — no model, no activations — reaches 0.93-0.95 on the same contrasts and **1.000** on
  the legal hard bands;
- an omnibus bag-of-words model reaches **0.96-1.00** on every v1 topic and scope.

**Three attempts to build a lexically-controlled instrument failed in three different ways** (Amendment 6). Draft 1
controlled pronouns and exposed a directive-modal cue at 0.875. Draft 2 controlled modals to exactly 0.500 and exposed a
professional-register cue — `clinicians`, `guidelines`, `pharmacists` in 28 of 40 information cases against 8 of 40
advice cases — at omnibus 1.000. Draft 3 controlled register by building 40 minimal pairs and passed every cue check
(pronoun 0.544, modal 0.545, professional-noun 0.500, length 0.485, omnibus leave-pair-out 0.478 inside a
within-pair-swap null of 0.435 +/- 0.084) — but dissolved the labels: after removing pronouns, articles and copulas the
two members of a pair are 0.814 similar at the median, 10 of 40 above 0.90, and `p11`, `p13` and `p16` are mislabelled
under the rule. In those pairs the only reliable difference between the classes is the pronouns.

**The finding, stated for the paper:** *in short authored medical text, directive force is substantially lexically
constituted — the surface cue and the semantic construct coincide at this granularity.* Every attempt to drive the
lexical baseline to chance drove label validity down with it, because the markers being balanced away are what make a
sentence directive. The consequence generalises beyond this probe: **short-text authored evaluation cannot certify a
semantic claim for any classifier.** Certifying one requires naturally occurring multi-sentence responses where
directive force is carried structurally across the discourse. Draft 3 is retained in the repository as an artifact of
this finding, marked `NOT FOR EVALUATION`; it was never scored.

**Practical implication, flagged for the paper:** for short responses a lexical classifier may be an adequate compliance
control, and any probe advantage would have to live in longer discourse-level text — the naturalistic evaluation that
would decide this is **future work, scoped as post-submission**.

**Methodological note for the protocol section.** Leave-one-out CV is invalid on minimal-pair data: the held-out case's
twin sits in the training fold carrying the opposite label, the model anti-predicts, and the run reports an extreme
inverted score — we measured 0.019. The correct procedure, now the standard, is leave-pair-out CV scored against a
within-pair-swap null.
